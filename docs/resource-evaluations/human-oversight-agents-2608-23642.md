# AI agents push humans out of the loop

**Reviewed:** 2026-09-27. **Score:** 4/5 for framing and proposed evaluation criteria. **Decision:** integrate into existing supervision and learning sections, without claiming a validated intervention.

**Source:** Margaret Mitchell, Avijit Ghosh and Samir Passi, [AI Agents Push Humans Out of the Loop, v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23642v3), September 6, 2026.

## Contribution and boundary

This is a position paper connecting research on automation and human-computer interaction to agent oversight. It argues that the conditions needed for meaningful supervision deserve design attention alongside agent capabilities. It is not a new randomized study measuring a universal cognitive decline, and it does not validate UVAL or this guide's exercises.

The source motivates attention to decision design, accessible evidence, bounded autonomy and opportunities to exercise judgment. Our proposed adaptation is a compact decision dossier and an exercise in finding evidence, challenging misleading advice and resuming a new task. Those adaptations still require local observation with real participants.

[Johan Martinsson's account of reviewing AI PRs](https://unlockers.ai/blog/relire-des-pr-faites-par-l-ia) supplies a complementary practitioner question: what work remains enjoyable and valuable when generation expands but review remains human? It is a field account, not an industry-wide productivity or clinical measurement.

## Editorial decisions

- Correct UVAL passages that infer learning from a cosmetic edit or promise retention without observations. Correct code may remain unchanged.
- Remove universal review line/time ceilings and unsupported causal interpretations. Calibrate workload and verification capacity locally.
- Keep decision correctness, ability to resume, human effort and satisfaction separate. Faster approval and lower disagreement can have several explanations.
- Preserve an initial judgment before advice on selected exercises; do not impose duplicate human approval on every routine action.

The technical challenge identified a mislabeled Sonar toil measure, a nested denominator that the survey did not state, and obligations incorrectly inferred from an FDA draft. The affected learning-guide passages were corrected. This scoped review does not certify every historical claim in that page.
